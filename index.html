Yara M. Damaj

Political Theorist

Yara M. Damaj is a political theorist and Postdoctoral Fellow at the Middle East Center at the University of Pennsylvania, where she is completing her first book manuscript, Dispossession by Nostalgia. Her research examines how affect, popular culture, and memory operate as political forces, with particular attention to the attachments that bind people to political arrangements even when those arrangements reproduce the very conditions of their dispossession.

Yara is currently developing articles on humor as a political form under conditions of crisis and on the colonial language of kinship through which France was cast as Lebanon’s "tender mother," a figure she examines in relation to Maronite elite claims to whiteness. Her earlier work on the sentimental and aesthetic dimensions of Islamic State media appeared in the International Journal of Communication.

She holds a PhD in Political Science from the University of Pennsylvania, where she also earned a Graduate Certificate in Gender, Sexuality, and Women’s Studies. Her doctoral research was supported by the Benjamin Franklin Fellowship, the Boies Family Fellowship, the Samuel H. Preston Fellowship, and a graduate fellowship at the Andrea Mitchell Center for the Study of Democracy, with additional support from the Center for the Study of Ethnicity, Race, and Immigration and the Christopher H. Browne Center for International Politics. She received the Alvin Z. Rubinstein Award for Excellence in Teaching by a Graduate Student, as well as multiple leadership and service awards as a Graduate Resident Advisor. Yara also holds an MA in Public Policy and International Affairs and a BA in Political Studies and Psychology from the American University of Beirut.
